British Gas Complaint? Here's What You Can Do (2025/2026 Guide)

Overcharged on your bill, ignored after raising a complaint, or stuck with a malfunctioning smart meter? British Gas received over 142,000 complaints in Q1 2026 alone. You are not on your own — and you have more rights than they want you to know about.

Why British Gas Complaints Are So Common

British Gas is the UK's largest domestic energy supplier, which means it also generates more complaints than almost any other. Their own published data shows 142,860 complaints received in the first quarter of 2026 alone — and over 7,400 of those were escalated to the Energy Ombudsman in the same period.

The issues that come up repeatedly are not random. They follow a pattern. Billing errors caused by a flawed new billing system introduced in 2023. Direct debits raised without adequate notice or justification. Smart meters that stop transmitting readings — followed by estimated bills that bear no relation to actual usage. Credit balances sitting on accounts that British Gas is slow to return. And a customer service operation that many customers describe as difficult to reach, inconsistent in its responses, and prone to closing complaints before they are resolved.

British Gas has paid out millions in regulatory fines and customer redress over these issues. That matters, because it confirms the conduct is real, documented, and actionable.

The Most Common British Gas Complaints

1. Incorrect or Inflated Bills

Customers report receiving bills based on estimated readings that are significantly higher than actual usage — in some cases, more than double. Others have been placed on incorrect meter records after switching, resulting in bills that don't reflect the energy they actually used. British Gas has acknowledged systemic billing errors linked to their billing platform update, and Ofgem has intervened on multiple occasions.

2. Direct Debits Raised Without Justification

A large number of complaints relate to direct debits being increased without adequate explanation, or variable direct debits that bear no relationship to actual consumption. In some cases, customers have been double-charged in the same month due to account setup errors — money taken and not promptly returned.

3. Smart Meter Failures

An estimated four million smart meters across the UK are not functioning correctly. British Gas customers report meters that go "dumb" — stopping transmitting readings — sometimes for months or even years. During that period, bills revert to estimates. Customers trying to switch supplier are often told they cannot do so until the meter issue is resolved, effectively trapping them with British Gas.

4. Credit Balance Refusals

If you have overpaid, British Gas holds a credit balance on your account. Many customers report difficulty getting that money returned. Under Ofgem's rules, energy suppliers must repay credit balances promptly on request. Delays or refusals are not acceptable — and are a basis for formal complaint.

5. Complaints Closed Without Resolution

Multiple customers report raising formal complaints, receiving a reference number, and then finding the complaint marked as resolved or closed without the underlying issue being fixed. This is a documented tactic across large energy suppliers and one that the Energy Ombudsman takes seriously.

Your Rights as a British Gas Customer

You have clear, enforceable rights under UK law:

  • Consumer Rights Act 2015 — services must be performed with reasonable care and skill. Persistent billing errors and unresolved complaints are a failure to meet this standard.
  • Supply of Goods and Services Act 1982 — services must be carried out within a reasonable time. Months of unresolved issues is not reasonable.
  • Ofgem's Standards of Conduct — British Gas must treat you fairly, communicate clearly, and process complaints properly.
  • Energy Ombudsman access — if British Gas has not resolved your complaint within eight weeks, or has issued a final position letter, you are entitled to escalate to the Energy Ombudsman at no cost to you. The Ombudsman can compel refunds, compensation payments, and written apologies.
  • Credit balance refunds — under Ofgem rules, British Gas must refund credit balances promptly on request. You do not need to accept delays.

What To Do — Step by Step

1. Put Your Complaint in Writing

Do not rely on phone calls. Email or write to British Gas and state the problem clearly, referencing relevant dates, amounts, and account details. A written record is your evidence.

2. Set a 14-Day Deadline for Resolution

State in your correspondence that you expect a substantive response within 14 days. This is the standard pre-action timeframe and signals you are prepared to escalate.

3. Reference the Relevant Legislation

Citing the Consumer Rights Act 2015 and Ofgem's Standards of Conduct in your letter demonstrates you understand your position. Companies respond differently to customers who demonstrate legal awareness.

4. If No Resolution — Escalate to the Energy Ombudsman

The Energy Ombudsman is free, independent, and has binding powers over British Gas. After eight weeks without resolution (or following a final position letter), you can file. The Ombudsman can award compensation, order refunds, and require British Gas to take specific action.

5. Consider Small Claims Court for Financial Losses

If you have suffered a quantifiable financial loss — an overcharge you cannot recover, a credit balance withheld, consequential costs — the Small Claims Court is a realistic and relatively straightforward option for claims under £10,000.

When You Should Act Quickly

Do not wait if any of the following apply:

  • You are being chased for a debt you do not believe you owe
  • A credit marker or debt has been placed on your credit file incorrectly
  • You have a significant credit balance that British Gas is refusing to return
  • Your smart meter has stopped working and your bills are based on estimates
  • You are approaching the eight-week window for Ombudsman escalation
  • The billing error is affecting your ability to switch supplier
