Your Deposit Is Missing — Here's Exactly What To Do (2025/2026 Guide)

If your landlord hasn't protected your deposit, they have broken the law — and you are entitled to claim compensation of up to three times the deposit amount. You do not need a solicitor. Here is exactly how to do it.

What the Law Requires

In England and Wales, landlords taking a deposit on an assured shorthold tenancy are legally required to do two things within 30 days of receiving the money:

  • Register the deposit with one of three government-approved tenancy deposit protection schemes
  • Provide the tenant with written "prescribed information" confirming where the deposit is held and how disputes are handled

These obligations come from the Housing Act 2004, amended by the Localism Act 2011 and Deregulation Act 2015. They are not optional. Failure on either count — protecting the deposit, or providing the prescribed information — is a breach of the law and gives you the right to claim compensation.

The Renters' Rights Act 2025, which came into force in 2026, has made this even more significant. With Section 21 "no-fault" evictions now abolished, a landlord who has failed to protect your deposit is in a considerably weaker legal position than ever before.

Check All Three Schemes in 5 Minutes

There are three government-approved deposit protection schemes in England and Wales. Your deposit must be registered with one of them. Checking all three takes under five minutes:

  1. Deposit Protection Service (DPS) — depositprotection.com
  2. MyDeposits — mydeposits.co.uk
  3. Tenancy Deposit Scheme (TDS) — tenancydepositscheme.com

On each site, search using your name, your rental address, and the dates of your tenancy. If nothing comes back on any of the three, your deposit is unprotected. Take screenshots of all three search results. Those screenshots are your evidence.

If your landlord claims the deposit is protected but cannot provide a certificate, that is itself a red flag. Request the certificate in writing. A landlord with a properly protected deposit can produce that document in minutes.

What You Are Entitled To Claim

If your deposit was not protected within 30 days, or the prescribed information was not provided, a court can order your landlord to pay you between one and three times the deposit amount — in addition to returning the deposit itself.

  • 1x the deposit — where there was a technical breach but the landlord has since corrected it
  • 2x or 3x the deposit — where there was a deliberate failure, a significant delay, or the landlord has not remedied the situation

On a £1,200 deposit, that is a potential claim of up to £3,600 in compensation, plus the return of the deposit itself. The court fee for filing a small claim is currently £377, which is recoverable from the landlord if you win.

On a joint tenancy, each tenant can bring a separate claim. The exposure for a landlord with multiple tenants on an unprotected deposit rises sharply.

What To Do — Step by Step

1. Confirm the Breach in Writing

Email your landlord or letting agent asking them to confirm the name of the scheme holding your deposit, the scheme reference number, and to provide a copy of the prescribed information. Keep this email. If they cannot answer these questions, you have confirmed the breach on the record.

2. Send a Letter Before Action

Before making a court claim you must send a formal letter before action. This sets out the breach, states the compensation you are seeking, and gives the landlord a fixed period — typically 14 days — to respond or settle. Most landlords settle at this stage. They know the law, and they know that going to court makes their position worse.

This is the letter we provide via our templates service, or draft on your behalf as part of our dispute service.

3. File via the Small Claims Court if Needed

If the landlord does not respond or refuses to settle, you can file a claim online through the HMCTS Money Claims portal. You do not need a solicitor. The process is designed for individuals to use without legal representation. The court fee is currently £377 and is recoverable if you win.

4. Use the Deposit Scheme's Dispute Service for Withheld Deposits

If your deposit was protected but your landlord is now refusing to return it or making deductions you consider unfair, each scheme has its own free Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) service. This is separate from a compensation claim for non-protection — it covers disputes about the amount being returned at the end of the tenancy.

When to Act Quickly

Do not wait if any of the following apply:

  • Your tenancy has ended and the deposit has not been returned
  • Your landlord is making deductions you were not told about at check-in
  • You have been served with a notice to leave and your deposit was never protected
  • Your landlord is disputing that a deposit was taken, despite your bank records showing otherwise
  • You are in a joint tenancy and only one tenant's deposit appears to be protected

There is a six-year time limit on deposit protection claims, but the stronger your position, the earlier you act. A landlord facing a court claim moves faster when the breach is recent and well-documented.
